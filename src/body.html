How to read these columns
Students
Public school students living in the district's towns, 2024–25 (VCGI). This is a headcount by town of residence, not the Agency of Education's average daily membership (ADM) that the law uses, so expect differences of a few percent.
Since 2014
Change in the same count from 2014–15.
Span
Straight-line distance between the two farthest town centers, in miles. Road distance is longer.
GL / student
Equalized education grand list (Act 73 basis) divided by students: the property wealth behind each student. vs state compares it with the statewide figure.
Child pov.
Children 5–17 below the poverty line, ACS 2020–2024. Small towns have wide margins of error; district totals are steadier.
Pub. HS, Ind. HS
Public schools with grades 9–12 inside the district, and independent schools with grades 9–12 (including the historic academies many towns tuition to).
Small
Public schools with fewer than 100 students.
Pieces
More than 1 means the district is split into parts that don't touch.
SUs
How many of today's supervisory unions have towns in this district.

Where things stand

Act 73 of 2025 asked a Redistricting Task Force for up to three statewide maps of larger districts. The Task Force's December 2025 report declined to recommend forced mergers. In early 2026 the House and Senate Education Committees each drew a map: the House draft (27 supervisory districts of about 2,000 to 4,000 students) and the Senate's version 6 (12 new supervisory unions plus a "grey area" left as is). Both are loaded as starting points. The Legislature then passed H.955, signed June 18 as Act 170, which makes mergers voluntary but required to be studied: it puts every supervisory union into one of seven Cooperative Educational Service Areas and lists 20 suggested groupings for merger committees. Those are loaded too.

The State's own builder at map.vermont.gov/education/district-builder (VCGI) also groups towns into districts. This one adds side-by-side comparison with today's map, tax-capacity and poverty measures, contiguity checks and an auto-balancer.

How it works

Towns are the building blocks: you move whole towns between districts, and the schools in a town move with it. Today's map is the 52 supervisory unions and supervisory districts, since those are the governance units the consolidation debate is about. Three of them are already in pieces on the map (Kingdom East, Orange East, Montpelier Roxbury).

The scorecard compares your plan with today's map:

  • In the size target: districts whose student count is inside the target you pick on the Plan tab.
  • Grand list per student, highest ÷ lowest: how far apart the richest and poorest districts are in property wealth per student. Lower is more even.
  • Students near the state's tax capacity: share of students in districts within 25% of the statewide grand list per student.
  • Child poverty segregation: Theil's H for children 5–17 in and not in poverty across districts (0 = every district has the state's rate).
  • Longest span: the largest straight-line distance between two town centers in one district.
  • Without a public high school: districts with no public school serving grades 9–12. Many such towns tuition students to independent academies, shown separately in the Districts tab.
  • SUs split: today's supervisory unions whose towns end up in more than one district.

Auto-balance is simulated annealing: it tries thousands of single-town moves along borders and keeps those that improve a weighted score, never breaking a district into pieces. Generated examples are one possible map each, not the best or only answer.

Data

  • Towns, students by town of residence (2014–15 to 2024–25), equalized education grand list, supervisory unions, regions: VCGI's VT_School_Geo.json, the dataset behind the State's builder.
  • Schools, enrollment, grades, year built: VCGI schools_all.json (2024–25).
  • Town boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary files, county subdivisions, 2024; lakes from TIGER/Line area water.
  • Child poverty: American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, table B17001.
  • House draft map: school-district list in the Joint Fiscal Office's March 11, 2026 analysis. Senate map: the district-builder link in Senate Ed Map Information, version 6.
  • CESAs and merger groupings: Act 170 of 2026, Secs. 2 and 14.

Limits

  • Student counts are by town of residence, not ADM, and include tuition students wherever they attend.
  • Merger groupings in Act 170 list school districts; here they are drawn by town. Wells, Middletown Springs, Stratton, Northfield and Williamstown follow their school district rather than their SU.
  • The House draft lists school districts; Quarry Valley and Montpelier Roxbury appear under two numbers, so Poultney is placed with Slate Valley and Roxbury with Barre. With those placements, every district's student count is within 4% of JFO's ADM.
  • Rivendell and SAU 70 are interstate districts; only their Vermont towns appear.
  • Grand list values are on the Act 73 basis used by VCGI and will differ from current tax-year figures.
